TOPIC 1: Market Awareness & Progressive Exposure
“There are times to go long, times to go short, and times to go fishing.” – Jesse Livermore
The 6-Gear Progressive Braking System & Risk Per Trade (RPT) Matrix:
The Estate's exposure is dynamically throttled based on a 6-Gear consensus model evaluating Large Caps (SPY), Tech/Growth (QQQ), Market Breadth (RSP), and Implied Volatility (VIX). We utilize the 20-day and 50-day Simple Moving Averages (SMA) for rapid risk mitigation.
•	Gear 5 (Deep Green / Complacent): SPY, QQQ, and RSP are ALL > 20 SMA. VIX is < 15.
o	RPT: 0.25% NAV (1R). Max aggression.
•	Gear 4 (Light Green / Normal): SPY, QQQ, and RSP are ALL > 20 SMA. VIX is between 15 and 18.
o	RPT: 0.25% NAV (1R). Standard baseline.
•	Gear 3 (Yellow-Green / Healthy Pullback): Indices are mixed (some < 20 SMA), but ALL are > 50 SMA. VIX is < 18.
o	RPT: 0.125% NAV (0.5R). Half-size risk.
•	Gear 2 (True Yellow / Chop & Fear): SPY, QQQ, and RSP are ALL > 50 SMA. VIX is between 18 and 22.
o	RPT: 0.125% NAV (0.5R). Only Playbook A (Catalyst/Episodic Pivot) trades authorized.
•	Gear 1 (Orange / Structural Warning): ONE index breaks < 50 SMA, but the others hold. VIX is < 22.
o	RPT: 0.05% NAV (0.2R). Scouting positions only. Testing the waters with fractional capital.
•	Gear 0 (Red / Black Swan): Multiple indices < 50 SMA. OR VIX is > 22.
o	RPT: 0.00% NAV (0R). Alpha Engine is HALTED. Cash and Tail Hedges only.

The Sector Veto (Micro-Weather Override):
A rising tide does not lift all boats. Before executing a trade, the target stock's specific Sector ETF (e.g., XLK, KRE, XLE) must be evaluated. If the Sector ETF is trading below its 50-day SMA, the Alpha Risk Calculator will mechanically apply a penalty, dropping your allowable risk by one full gear (e.g., a Gear 4 environment drops to a Gear 3 risk allowance for that specific stock). You are only as strong as your weakest link.

The Tiered Drawdown Governor (The Rubber Band Model):
The Estate does not use arbitrary time-based lockouts. Instead, we use a dynamic multiplier based on the portfolio's current drawdown from its High-Water Mark (HWM). This mathematically prevents ruin while allowing for compounding recovery. The multiplier applies to BOTH your Risk Per Trade (RPT) and your Total Open Risk (TOR):
•	Tier 1 (Peak Performance): 0.0% to -1.0% Drawdown ➡️ 1.0x Multiplier (Full Size)
•	Tier 2 (Caution Zone): -1.0% to -2.0% Drawdown ➡️ 0.5x Multiplier (Half Size)
•	Tier 3 (Danger Zone): -2.0% to -3.0% Drawdown ➡️ 0.25x Multiplier (Quarter Size)
•	Tier 4 (System Lockout): Worse than -3.0% Drawdown ➡️ 0.0x Multiplier (Alpha Engine Halted)
The Squeeze: If a drawdown pushes the portfolio into a lower tier, the Global TOR ceiling instantly drops. If current open risk exceeds the new ceiling, the manager is strictly forbidden from opening new trades and must actively raise stops or trim positions to get back under the limit. Recovery is organic; as winning trades push the NAV back up through the tiers, the multiplier expands.

TOPIC 3: The Virtual CFO Risk Doctrine
Risk is measured in "R", not shares.
Rule 1: The Global High-Water Mark (HWM) Risk Peg
Alpha trading is a privilege funded by the Estate's overall growth. Because all strategies impact the bottom line, the Alpha budget is dictated strictly by the Global Estate NAV.
•	The HWM Anchor: The Estate tracks the highest Global NAV ever achieved (adjusted for external cash deposits and withdrawals).
•	The Absolute Drawdown Limit: The Alpha Engine is granted a strict maximum drawdown allowance of 3.0% from the HWM.
•	Total Open Risk (TOR) Cap: The Estate's maximum allowable Total Open Risk at any given moment is strictly capped at 10x the active regime's Risk Per Trade (RPT). (e.g., In Gear 5 at Tier 1, max RPT is 0.20%, meaning Global TOR is capped at 2.0%).
•	The Hard Lock (Tier 4): If the portfolio draws down more than 3.0% from the HWM, the Alpha Engine is HALTED. Risk 'R' is financed only by overall Estate gains. The engine remains locked until passive yield (IB01) or VRP income organically pushes the Global NAV back up.

Rule 2: The Sector Correlation Cap
•	The Estate may hold a maximum of 1 active campaign in the same sector (e.g., max 1 Metals/Mining stocks). This prevents a single macroeconomic headline from causing a cascading drawdown.

Rule 3: The Velocity Cap (Max New Trades)
•	The Estate is strictly limited to opening a maximum of 2 new Alpha Campaigns per day during Green Regimes (Gears 4 & 5).
•	The Yellow/Orange Choke: During Gear 1, 2, or 3, this limit is mechanically choked to a maximum of 1 new Alpha Campaign per day to prevent shotgunning capital into choppy markets.
•	Why: This protects the portfolio from single-day correlation flushes and forces ruthless prioritization of the Wait List. If you have 5 valid setups on your staging pipeline, you cannot buy them all; you are forced to deploy capital only into the absolute best setups, naturally elevating the quality of the Estate's holdings.


Rule 4: The Absolute Notional Cap (The Gap-Down Failsafe)
Risk is not just the distance to your stop-loss; it is the total capital exposed to an overnight gap-down. No single Alpha position may ever exceed 5% of the Global Estate's Notional NAV, regardless of how tight the technical stop-loss is.

Rule 5: The IPO & Unproven Asset Quarantine
Any asset with less than 6 months of public trading history, or a market capitalization under $2 Billion, is classified as "Unproven." These assets are strictly capped at a maximum of 0.5R risk and a 2% Notional Cap.

Rule 6: The Earnings Blackout
We do not hold full Alpha positions through earnings announcements. If a stock is approaching earnings, the position must be closed or trimmed to a negligible "lotto" size the day prior.

Rule 7: The Integrity Violation (The Stop-Loss Ratchet)
A Stop-Loss can only move UP. Lowering a stop to give a stock "more room" is an Integrity Violation and is strictly banned.

Rule 8: Scaling In vs. Averaging Down
•	Tranche Accumulation: If buying a dip, the system will calculate exactly how high the Stop-Loss MUST be raised so that the Total Campaign Risk never exceeds the Regime's RPT limit.
•	Pro-Trend Pyramiding: If adding to a winner, we raise the Stop-Loss to lock in profit on the first tranche and use the newly freed-up "R" to finance the risk of the second tranche.

Rule 9: Portfolio Management (The "Farm System")
The Alpha portfolio operates on a two-tier system to manage mental bandwidth and risk.
1) The Minor Leagues (Scouting): Up to 10-15 positions sized at a fraction of risk (e.g., 0.1R) with wide structural stops to test thesis viability.
2) The Major Leagues (Core Alpha): A strict maximum of 3 to 5 positions. These are the scouts that proved their thesis and broke out. They are sized up to the full 1R limit with tightly trailed stops.

Rule 10: Liquidity & Penny Stock Caps
•	The ADV Rule: A position size must never exceed 1% of the stock's Average Daily Volume (ADV).
•	The Small Cap Trap: For stocks priced under $10.00, the maximum allowable risk is strictly capped at 0.5R (half-size), regardless of the macro regime. This protects the Estate from the severe percentage slippage inherent in single-digit equities.

Rule 11: The Silo B "Prop Desk" Exemption (The Satellite)
To pursue outsized, triple-digit annual returns (e.g., Qullamaggie/Minervini breakout strategies) without endangering the generational wealth of the Estate, Silo B is officially designated as an isolated Proprietary Trading Desk (The Satellite).
•	The Risk Quarantine: The entirety of Silo B's Net Liquidation (roughly 2.5% to 3.0% of the Global Estate) is considered the maximum risk. If Silo B goes to zero, the Global Estate survives.
•	The Exemption: Inside Silo B, the standard v7.1 macro handcuffs are removed. The manager is authorized to risk 1.0% to 1.5% of Silo B's localized NAV per trade, and may concentrate up to 25% of Silo B's capital into a single high-conviction stock.
•	The Psychological Mandate: The manager must accept that Silo B will experience violent -30% localized drawdowns in pursuit of +150% gains. Silo B capital must never be commingled with the macro risk math of Silos A and C.


TOPIC 4: The Active Entry Playbooks (Intraday & EOD)
Because we are actively managing the portfolio, we utilize precision intraday execution to secure optimal swing entries. We do not day trade, but we execute like snipers.
The Pre-Flight Checklist & ATR Floor
1. The Multi-Timeframe ATR Floor (Volatility-Adjusted Sizing):
A stop-loss must give the asset mathematical room to breathe. The width of the stop-loss is dynamically linked to the VIX, the current Market Gear, and the Trade Horizon.
•	The Horizon Matching Rule: The ATR timeframe must match the intended holding period.
o	Short-Term Swings: Use Daily ATR.
o	Medium-Term Holds: Use Weekly ATR.
o	Long-Term Core: Use Monthly ATR.
•	Gear 1 & 2 (High Chop / VIX 18-22): Stop-loss must be at least 2.0x the selected timeframe's ATR. Wide stops, smaller share size to survive whipsaws.
•	Gear 3 & 4 (Normal / VIX 15-18): Stop-loss must be at least 1.5x the selected timeframe's ATR. Standard baseline.
•	Gear 5 (Complacent / VIX < 15): Stop-loss may be tightened to 1.25x the selected timeframe's ATR, or placed at the Prior Day Low (PDL). Tight stops, larger share size to maximize the bull run.

2.	The Thesis Mandate: A ticker cannot be promoted to "Armed" in the Staging Pipeline unless the manager has selected at least one "Why" tag, one "Where" tag, and logged a written thesis into the database. Blind gambling is prohibited.

Playbook A: The Episodic Pivot (Q-EP)

Playbook B: The Champion Base & Structural Bounce

•	The Setup: A Stage 2 stock breaking out of a Volatility Contraction Pattern (VCP), or a high-conviction stock pulling back to a rising moving average.
•	Execution: Can be executed via resting EOD orders (Buy Stop Limit above the previous day's high) OR via live intraday alerts when the stock crosses the structural pivot.
•	The Stop: Placed safely below the moving average or the recent contraction pivot.

Playbook C: The Social Alpha Filter (Trust but Verify)
•	Tags: Social_Alpha.
•	The Filter: The tip must be backed by a logical thesis AND must be interacting with a valid technical support level (Playbook A or B). We do not buy blindly into the middle of a chart with no structural support to hide our stop-loss behind.


TOPIC 5: The Mechanical Exit Protocol
Entries are discretionary and active; exits are purely mechanical.
•	Phase 1: Survival & The Day 1 Cushion (Days 1-4)
o	The Hard Stop: A physical stop-loss is always resting in the market to protect against intraday flash crashes.
o	The Day 1 EOD Rule (The "Squat" Rule): If a newly triggered position is negative (red) 15 minutes before the market close on the exact day of entry, close the position manually. Do not hold Day 1 losers overnight. If the breakout fails to hold its gains by the closing bell, the institutional thesis is invalid. Cut it for a fractional loss.
•	Phase 1b: The "Dead Asset" Time Stop (Day 10)
Capital velocity is paramount. If an Alpha campaign has not reached its +1R "Free Trade" pivot within 10 trading days, it is mechanically closed at the current market price. If the thesis has not materialized in two weeks, the setup is invalid. Free the capital.
•	Phase 2: The "Free Trade" Pivot (At +1R Profit)
o	The moment the stock price reaches a +1R gain, the Hard Stop is immediately moved to Breakeven (Cost + Commissions). The trade now has Zero Open Risk.
•	Phase 3: The Time Exit (Day 5)
o	Time > Price. If the stock has not stopped us out by Day 5, we mechanically sell 30% to 50% of the position. This finances the risk and clears mental capital.
•	Phase 4: The Trend Trail (Day 6+)
o	The remaining 50% to 70% is left to catch the macro run. We trail the Stop-Loss using the 20-day SMA (normal trends) or the 50-day SMA (macro trends). We exit when the stock definitively closes below the moving average.

•	The Supernova Override (Windfall Clause):
o	If a stock surges +3R or more in a single day, normal trailing rules are suspended.
o	Immediately move the Hard Stop to 50% of that day’s candle body.
o	If the price breaks the Prior Day Low (PDL) the following day, exit the position immediately. Do not let a parabolic windfall turn into a round-trip.
