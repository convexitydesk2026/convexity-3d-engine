## Substack Article: Pyramiding (Aug 20, 2026)
**Core Philosophy & The Holy Grail:**
- Pyramiding (selective amplification of highest-conviction trades) is a proven strategy used by legends like Soros, Druckenmiller, and Kovner to amass fortunes.
- **The Holy Grail of Diversification:** A portfolio does not need hundreds of assets to be diversified. It is better to hone in on best-in-class ideas to create outperformance. Pyramiding acts as a secondary layer of sizing/conviction.
- **Risk vs. Position Size:** Top trend-following contrarians pick strong initial entries with only 1-2% account risk. As the trade moves in their favor, they build up the position size. The position size might become massive, but the actual *risk* remains controlled (because stops are trailed up).

**The Mechanics & Expected Value:**
- The concept is simple: add to winners, cut losers, and hold for an outsized winner/loser imbalance.
- The difficulty lies in managing dynamic win rates and understanding the trend cycle.
- **Expected Value Math:** Pyramiding must balance the odds: EV = P(right) * R(right) - P(wrong) * R(wrong).
- **When to Pyramid:** The stronger the trend and the younger the trade is in its overall expected range, the higher the chance of successful pyramiding. The goal is to add on consolidations where the odds of continuation remain high but the technical structure allows risk (the stop loss) to be tightly managed.
**The Art of Pyramiding & Identifying Opportunities:**
- The hardest part is waiting for the *big ideas* (massive range potential of 200%+) with strong certainty of a long-term bottom, low volatility, clear impulse legs, and growing fundamentals.
- **Wall St Cheat Sheet Anchor:** He looks for assets in the "Disbelief" and "Depression" phases to anchor the asset to a high-likelihood bottoming area.
- **Ascending Ranges:** When pyramiding in a range/consolidation, the range MUST be ascending. An ascending bottom principle reduces the risk of getting chopped up (ping pong) between the top and bottom of the range.
- **ETFs:** ETFs have a special place in this approach. The risk of a single asset imploding is mitigated, increasing overall path certainty (though at the cost of some volatility due to index components).

**The 1 Million Percent Return in 2020:**
- His success in 2020 came because the *opportunity set tripled*, the *ranges doubled*, but the *destination remained the same*.
- He had years of data on his bucket of stocks, giving him confidence in the ultimate destination even if he had no control over the volatile path to get there.
- Increased volatility hurt his *entry win rate* (he could be wrong on the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd entry), but it did NOT change the underlying odds of the setup.
- **Crucial Insight on Setup Probability vs. Entry Win Rate:** If a setup historically has a 75% probability of resolving in your direction, getting stopped out 3 times on entries are NOT three independent 75/25 bets. They are three attempts to access the *same* underlying 75% opportunity. Because the payoff became so much larger in 2020, risking small amounts on multiple attempts was mathematically superior.

**The Asymmetry of Entry Win Rate:**
- You can be repeatedly wrong about *where* the move begins, while ultimately being right about *where it ends*.
- For example: Attempt 1 (-1R), Attempt 2 (-1R), Attempt 3 (-1R), Attempt 4 (+16R). The entry win rate is a terrible 25%, but the net result is a massive +13R.
- Fröhlich views those first 3 losing attempts simply as the "cost of maintaining access to the opportunity."
- **Multiple Opportunities:** Having 4 or 5 valid setups triggers in a day (even if correlated) drastically increases the probability that at least ONE setup will give the clean path needed to deploy the pyramiding payoff.

**The Trade, Mechanically (The Math of Pyramiding):**
- **1R** = the risk on one unit from entry to stop.
- **The Trigger:** He enters one unit with a stop. He will then add another unit for every **2R** of favorable movement, provided the price structure allows it (consolidations/bases).
- **Trailing the Stop:** As tranches are added, the stop for the *entire* position is moved to approximately **1R behind price**, aligning with the new technical structure (e.g., just below the new higher low).
- **Financing Risk:** Every time price travels another 2R and a tranche is added, the older tranches have accumulated another +2R while the new trailed stop gives back only ~1R. The existing position increasingly finances the next addition.
- **Breakeven on the Sequence:** By the second addition (3 units total), the locked profit on the winning trade (+3R) entirely covers the losses from the first 3 failed attempts (-3R). From that point on, the entire sequence (including past stop-outs) cannot lose money.
- **The Final Exit:** If the move reaches a theoretical +7R target, the final realized gain across all 4 units is **+16R**. Netting out the 3 previous failed attempts, the trader walks away with +13R net sequence profit.

**Target Sell vs. Trailing Stop:**
- Selling into a predefined target captures the full range (+13R net) but requires massive destination conviction.
- Trailing the stop gives back roughly 1R per unit (+9R net in the example) but keeps you in the trade if the move runs spectacularly past the target (the 95/5 principle).
- **The Blend:** In practice, he blends the two approaches: he trims tranches into the target zone, and trails the rest.

**The Sizing Dial (How Much to Add):**
- **Equal-size adds (e.g., 1 + 1 + 1 + 1):** The neutral baseline that yields +16R on a full target hit.
- **Half-size adds (e.g., 1 + 0.5 + 0.5 + 0.5):** Yields less total R (+11.5R) but the average entry price remains much further away from the current price. Normal volatility has much more difficulty shaking you out. It "monetizes the certainty already earned."
- **1.5x adds (e.g., 1 + 1.5 + 1.5 + 1.5):** Yields massive returns (+20.5R) but "re-risks" the already-earned safety into greater exposure. You only do this when confidence in the remaining path is exceptionally high.
- **Pyramid Failure:** If volatility becomes larger than the structure used to protect the position, you reach maximum size exactly when the market shakes you out. You "sized for a clean path and got chop."

**From R to Account (Deriving the 1-2% Risk Rule):**
- R is just an abstraction until you map it to Account Equity. The legendary "1-2% risk" rule is not folklore; it is derived mathematically from the Kelly Criterion.
- **Key Insight:** You must apply the Kelly Criterion at the level of the *sequence*, not the individual entry. The unit of risk is the *maximum sequence loss* (e.g., -4R for 3 stopouts + 1 anchor).
- **The Math:** If Win Rate (p) = 0.75, and Payoff Ratio (b) = 13R net gain / 4R max sequence loss = 3.25. Full Kelly = 0.67 (Risking 67% of account per sequence).
- Full Kelly is insane and unlivable ("the ceiling of rationality, not a target"). 
- Taking **Quarter-Kelly** (17% of account per sequence), and dividing it by the 4R the sequence consumes, means **1R = ~4% of the account**.
- **Correlation Adjustment:** If you have 3 momentum tickers offering the exact same setup on the same day, they are NOT three independent sequences. They are "one sequence wearing three tickers." You must reduce the 4% risk proportionally based on correlation.

**What Pyramiding Is (The Mental Model):**
- 95% of gains come from 5% of positions. Pyramiding engineers this right-tail skew deliberately.
- It reduces your win rate by a few percentage points to exponentially increase your risk-reward. Failed adds are simply the price paid for the massive winners.
- **Trend vs. Capitulation:** Pyramiding works best in trend trades (where continuation confirms the thesis and odds improve) and poorly in capitulation trades (where EV peaks at the extreme and decays rapidly as the snapback happens).
- It acknowledges that the best performers go much further than expected, and your strategy must allow exposure to grow as that potential reveals itself.

3. **Pyramiding is the Holy Grail:** 95% of gains come from 5% of trades. Do not rely on diversification for alpha; rely on the selective amplification of your highest-conviction ideas.
4. **Pyramiding Requires Ascending Structure:** Only add tranches to a winning position if the technical structure (like higher lows or consolidations) allows you to trail the stop loss to protect the sequence. Never blindly average down.
5. **Entry Win Rate is an Illusion:** Taking three consecutive 1R losses to eventually catch a 16R winner is mathematically superior, even if the resulting "win rate" is a dismal 25%.
6. **Losses are the Cost of Access:** Early stop-outs on an anchor position shouldn't ruin your psychology. They are simply the necessary, calculated cost of maintaining access to a high-certainty destination.

8. **The Sizing Dial Tradeoff:** Adding smaller tranches on the way up protects your earned certainty. Adding larger tranches "re-risks" your profits to maximize upside, which should only be done when confidence in the remaining path is extreme.
9. **Sequence-Level Risk (Quarter-Kelly):** Derive your 1R risk mathematically, not anecdotally. Apply a fraction of the Kelly Criterion (like 1/4 Kelly) to the *maximum possible sequence loss* (e.g., 3 stops + 1 anchor) to determine your base 1R size.
