Trade overview: The great rotation
Few ideas and thoughts in regards to the trade landscape.
###
I was about to start a blog about the next batch of questions, but found myself researching quite deeply into certain ideas and market dynamics that I thought we would talk about first before continuing.

Some of you had been asking for more trade specific posts and I thought this would make for something interesting.

Dynamics
You can’t understand this landscape without money flows.

A trade idea, for me, starts much further back than the chart, at the macro, the big picture.

Where are people most long?

Where are people most short?

→ Where is the positioning?

What do people see as an easy long?

What do people see as obviously dead?

→ Where is sentiment best and worst?

→ And finally: what is nobody looking at at all?

So, where is the crowd very long, sentiment euphoric, and capital piling into the same corner?

Where are people very long, while sentiment is euphoric, where is the market consolidating capital?

Frankly pretty easy for all to know…


In Semiconductors.

BofA’s own Fund Manager Survey now has “long global semiconductors” tied for the single most crowded trade on the Street, the baton passed from long Mag 7 (a 54% reading at the peak) to gold, and now to semis. What unsettles me isn’t the reading itself, it’s where the crowd has packed in: not the broad FAANG cash machines of years past, but the single most cyclical, most capex-hostage corner of the whole complex.

We’ll walk a few positioning and sentiment reads and build an overall picture of the flows.

Semis are pressing against a historical barrier, the kind that marks oversaturation.

The top 10 names are now roughly 40% of the S&P 500, a record, about double where they sat a decade ago, and well above the dot-com peak, when the top 10 were around 23%. Read that twice: we are more concentrated today than we were in 2000. And that ~41% of the index’s weight is throwing off only about 32% of its earnings.

Every prior concentration felt safe too, the obvious place to be. Objectively, the concentration alone is a risk.
###
Flows have been incredible. AI, led by the US innovation created the biggest inflows into US stocks and more specifically Tech stocks ever, more than 2x what we experienced in any year of the past decade.
###
So the last several months have been about being in AI at all costs. The rotation out of literally anything else, plus the raw performance, pushed fund managers to capitulate, selling everything ex-AI to fund the AI bubble, the only way to have a chance against those that committed earlier, a fight for the AUM and cycle rippling through the investor base chasing like a snowball.

If you didn’t own AI or energy, you underperformed. Full stop.

In typical fashion, global energy equities topped around March 30, right on cue for quarter-end window dressing right as the war reached its most heated moments.

Energy had taken massive flows as the “logical” trade. Oil futures (CL1) topped about a week after the energy etf did.

Then the war bid came out. The “logical” trade everyone crowded near the top flipped to a full exit as oil rolled over. Long oil went from consensus to exited by the end of June, and it was literally the most crowded trade on the Street back in April.

Positioning walked away from energy, leaving AI as the only leg the market has to stand on.
###
AI wobbling?
It would be easy to recycle what we all already know: yes, inflows were historic, yes, AI is concentrated, in the US and in Korea alike. All true, all potentially priced in.

I want the points I’ve seen less of.

Start with the squeeze Chinese models are quietly creating.

The US leads frontier development. But it’s hit a wall it built itself: the most capable versions are now judged too dangerous, or too sensitive on export and security grounds, to fully release.

Fable got pulled; in my read, GPT-5.6 got shelved the same way. For the first time, the frontier can’t simply be shipped and monetized for everyone who wants it.

Meanwhile Chinese open models are taking token share and beating their US counterparts on cost. Most users have stopped caring, the models are good enough to lead even a step behind the frontier.
###
UBS reportedly found that around 60% of the enterprises it surveyed are now putting guardrails on AI spend, token cost and ROI have become budget-line problems, not press-release problems. Coinbase wrote a genuinely good piece on rewiring their stack for the same output at lower cost.

To me this is the biggest issue out there that nobody seems interested in.

Automatic routing, blending models per task, is still new. OpenRouter shipped Fusion a few weeks ago: models interacting, routing, and picking the best combination for the job.

That’s the shape of the next two years: Chinese models catch up and stay far cheaper, while US models sit capped at their “allowed” intelligence to limit security fallout.
###
Despite the massive slide on Chinese stocks due to economical reasons and stagnation in growth, specific spaces like AI are soaring higher.
###
The only reasons to stay on US models become security, network effects, and true frontier work. The rest of the usage drifts to Chinese models.

Mom and pop keep the easy app. But the coders and companies. where the tokens actually get spent, end up hybrid.

The moat around open-ended intelligence growth, the thing the whole US AI bid is priced on, cracked the day Fable got pulled, in my view.

Everything else: memory improvements, GPU optimization, the inference-cost curve, even the accounting games with circular capex-into-revenue, is second-order next to this. Bad for margins on their own, sure. But this dynamic attacks the moat itself, and whether the US side can ever monetize at all. The CAPEX bill is enormous; you need every dollar of cash flow just to be profitable, let alone to justify the multiple.
###
China is able to beat the US token usage at 1/10th of the cost.

The rotation
The most important thing to realize is that the Semis short is only part of the overall picture I see.

I see a market where owning anything other than AI led to pain, with energy being profitable for about 1 month and where people have capitulated to the hype.
###
Even the old FAANG crowding has thinned, that cohort pulled back ~15% and trades where it did back in September 2025.

The value in this trade is the rotation itself.

Selling the most crowded, most expensive corner of the market makes enormous room for everything else, at good-to-great prices, across a wide array of categories that have been starved for a year. Selling AI doesn’t destroy the money, it moves it, that is if the rotation isn’t followed by a recession, which I think would be a hard ask given the recent macro data.

Where are people giving up?
I’ll make the case for emerging markets.

At the start of the year, Trump’s opening moves had speculators heavily short the dollar, a short so crowded it put a floor under the dollar it couldn’t fall through.

Tariffs and the year’s actions then strengthened the dollar, which made foreign earnings worse in translation and put EM in the penalty box.

Underneath, the ground was shifting: country after country moved off the left toward pro-business, right-of-center governments: Argentina, Venezuela, Brazil into its coming election.

Now the positioning has done a full round trip. The same crowd that was wrong short the dollar in January is stretched the opposite way, long a stronger dollar and higher rates. The opposite of a year ago, and in my read, the same mistake pointed the other direction.

Oil is coming down. Truflation has inflation running near 2%. The Fed chair is being read as a hawk despite a Trump-given mandate and some quietly dovish comments. Every one of those cuts against the trade the crowd just piled into.

This is a recipe for easing hidden behind a curtain of momentary fear linked to the newest and first FED meeting led under Warsch as well as fers linked to the recent war. Both are behind us with inflation metrics and direct inflation drivers crashing. Right as the FED wanted to start on a hawkish tone to anchor inflation lower given the pressure markets wanted to put on Warsch and Trumps easing agenda.

Funny enough in my view trump wanted the impossible all this time of lower rates as inflation did not let him and right as the dynamic inverses the market has started to believe in higher for longer.
###
The cleanest picture of that extreme is in SOFR. Leveraged-fund shorts in SOFR futures hit a fresh record in the week to June 23, 2.97 million contracts, more than $700 billion notional, and more than double where they sat two months ago. The build came as firmer energy and inflation expectations forced a hawkish repricing of the Fed after the recent FOMC.

SOFR is the overnight funding rate, and its futures move inverse to expected short-term rates. So a short that size is one enormous bet that policy and funding costs stay higher-for-longer, or go higher still.

The whole leveraged crowd is now aligned behind one narrative. And at a record extreme, that’s exactly the setup for a violent short-covering reversal, if easing energy softens inflation expectations and takes the pressure off the Fed.

→ That’s the coiled spring, and a weaker dollar is the air for the EM trade.

In short I want to be long consumer, EM, growth… MELI
###
Emerging market bets: EWZ-Brazil ETF, ARGT-Argentina ETF and MELI

EWZ is the cleanest expression of the Brazil leg because the risk being repriced is not company-specific; it is the discount rate, the currency, and domestic financial conditions. The ETF still screens like a market priced for hesitation rather than recovery, roughly 10.5x earnings, 1.7x book, and a 4.6% trailing yield, with the sector mix concentrated in the areas most sensitive to a lower cost of capital: financials, materials, utilities, and energy.
###
Brazil is a major exporter, so it benefits from a weaker dollar, stronger commodity pricing, and improved EM flows.

The Lula reelection risk is already in the price in my view while the potential upside if the conservative party wins and the market begins considering a more business-friendly policy path is not.

Brazil is not a perfect macro story. Inflation is still above target and the central bank is cutting cautiously, with Selic now at 14.25%. But that is exactly the setup. If the dollar stops rising and the local rate path turns from headwind to tailwind, EWZ gives you liquid, high-beta exposure to a market where the multiple does not require much optimism to move.
###
The ETF is effectively a concentrated reform basket: MELI, YPF, Galicia, Vista, Banco Macro, Pampa and the other names that should benefit first if disinflation, reserve rebuilding, capital-market access, and policy credibility continue moving in the right direction. Inflation slowed to 2.1% month over month in May, while the IMF has cited stronger reform momentum, improved monetary and FX policy, and reserve accumulation under the current program.

The risks are obvious, which is why the convexity exists. The question is not whether Argentina has become boring. The question is whether the market has to start pricing even a path toward boring.

MELI is the quality anchor inside the trade. It gives me the LatAm consumer, payments, credit, logistics, advertising, and e-commerce penetration story without needing to underwrite one single country outcome.

The recent margin compression is the debate, but not necessarily the bear case. Q1 revenue and financial income grew 49% year over year to $8.8B, the fastest pace since Q2 2022, while operating margin fell to 6.9% as the company invested into free shipping, logistics, credit, and market-share gains. That is exactly the type of stock I want to watch if capital rotates out of U.S. AI: a regional compounder where the market is currently focused on near-term reinvestment pressure, while the underlying ecosystem keeps expanding.

So the structure is simple: EWZ for Brazil duration and currency beta, ARGT for Argentina reform convexity, and MELI for the regional quality compounder. If AI begins to leak capital and the dollar stops absorbing every marginal flow, this is where I think money can land first, not because the stories are clean, but because they are still under-owned, under-loved, and priced with far less perfection than the part of the market everyone just crowded into.

In short: Very low multiples, capitulation across nearly all categories in terms of positioning, lower forecast in yields/inflation and a potentially lower dollar right as money tries to rotate.

All in all
This is mainly a story about rotation.
Some spaces seem ready to turn, however it is the rotation itself, the shifting of capital that will set the plan on course.

I do not expect these trades to work if AI simply continues, we need people to search for alternatives and for capital to be redeployed.

In the past months cheap and getting cheaper meant a relative loss against the AI.

If AI fails short to mid term, cheap will become attractive once more.

This all starts with Korea panicking, which could topple SOXL, which then triggers flows.

It could be time for small caps, growth, EM and alike to outperform once more while the main indices, weighted through AI take a rest period.
