
## 3. The Breakout Strategy (Momentum Burst)
This is his secondary long strategy, which naturally follows the EP. He scans for the top 1% or 2% of stocks with the largest up-moves over the past 1, 3, and 6 months (i.e., established momentum leaders). 
*   **The Setup:** He looks for a short, tight consolidation (1 to 3 weeks) where volume dries up.
*   **The Trigger:** Entering on the breakout of that consolidation (buying strength above the opening range or 60-min high).

## Execution & Risk Management Rules
*   **The Neglected EP:** The absolute best Episodic Pivots happen on "neglected" stocks that have gone sideways for months or years and suddenly awaken on 10x volume (e.g., a massive earnings surprise on a beaten-down stock).
*   **Intraday Entry:** He does not buy blindly at the open. He waits for the stock to prove itself by taking out the high of the first 5-minute bar, or sometimes the first 60-minute bar. "I always buy into strength."
*   **The Hard Stop:** The stop loss is *always* the Low of the Day (LOD). 
*   **The ADTR Rule:** If the distance from his entry price to the Low of the Day is wider than the Average Daily True Range (ADTR), he *skips the trade*. A stop that wide ruins the Risk/Reward asymmetry.

## The Exit Strategy (Trailing the 10/20 SMA)
Kullamägi evolved his exits over time to capture massive trends:
*   **Step 1:** Take partial profits into the initial burst of strength (Days 3 to 5).
*   **Step 2:** Move the stop loss on the remaining position to Breakeven.
*   **Step 3:** Trail the rest of the position using a daily close below either the **10-day SMA** or **20-day SMA** (choosing the 10-day if the stock is extremely extended, or the 20-day for a slower trend).

## The Macro Filter (How He Lost $60 Million)
Unlike the Parabolic Short, EPs and Breakouts *require* a bullish market environment. 
*   **The Market Filter:** He uses the 10-day and 20-day SMAs on the broad market indices. If the 10-day crosses below the 20-day, it is a hard caution signal indicating a downtrend. You must stop buying breakouts.
*   **The $60M Lesson:** In 2021, Kullamägi's account peaked at $105 Million. By mid-2022, he drew down to $45 Million (a $60M loss). He admits this was entirely his fault because he was heavily leveraged long and *ignored his own 10/20 macro filter*, continuing to buy breakouts in a massive bear market.
*   **The Fix:** Now, if the broad market stalls and the 10-day SMA turns down, he aggressively sizes down and gets off margin immediately to protect his equity peak.

## The Mathematics of the Edge (Schwager's Take)
Jack Schwager and the authors highlight the underlying mathematics that make Kullamägi's system work:
*   **Win Rate is Irrelevant:** Kullamägi estimates his win rate is only between **25% and 30%**. Most of his trades are losers. This proves that an obsession with a high win rate is fundamentally wrongheaded. What matters is the expected value (Payoff Magnitude vs. Loss Magnitude).
*   **Position Sizing:** Despite turning thousands into millions, he typically risks **0.5% or less** of his account per trade, with an absolute maximum of **1%**. *This perfectly validates the use of a fixed, highly defensive risk calculator (like 0.10% NAV).* You do not need complex sequence math to achieve Wizard-level returns; you just need strict loss containment.
*   **The Double-Defense Mechanism:** His risk is contained by two mechanisms working together: 1) A restrictive position size (<1%), and 2) A massive, undeniable technical level (Low of Day) that prevents the loss from exceeding the planned size.

## The Psychology of a Wizard
*   **Conviction through Repetition:** He didn't invent any of his setups. He learned them from others (like Pradeep Bonde and William O'Neil). However, he built *unshakable conviction* by studying thousands of historical charts. If you don't put in the hours to verify the edge yourself, you will panic and abandon the rules the moment a trade goes against you.
*   **His Biggest Flaw:** He acknowledges that his greatest weakness is **overtrading**—losing patience and taking setups that do not fully meet his strict entry criteria. *The edge only exists if the strict criteria are met.*
*   **The "Peak Equity" Trap:** His worst drawdowns (like losing $60M) always occurred immediately following his greatest winning streaks. Massive success leads to complacency, which leads to ignoring the rules (like buying EPs during a macro downtrend).
## The Final Lesson: Resilience
Schwager concludes the chapter by pointing out that Kullamägi blew up three separate accounts early in his career, yet persevered to turn a $5,000 stake into tens of millions. The two ultimate takeaways for any aspiring trader are:
1. **Early failure does not necessarily imply longer-term failure.** (Your early drawdowns are the tuition you pay to the market).
2. **One of the critical qualities of Market Wizards is that they absolutely refuse to give up.**
